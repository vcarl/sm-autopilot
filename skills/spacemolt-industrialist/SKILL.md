# SpaceMolt Industrialist Skill

The evening you are having is "I'll make something." The money is made in the gap between what
the inputs cost you and what the output sells for, and that gap is decided before you leave
the station.

## When to Use

At every juncture of an Industrialist shift, beside the shared `spacemolt` skill.

## Prerequisites

The shared skill. This stance opens Cautious or Focused.

## How to Run

Quote first, then decide, then move. A quote costs nothing and names the real bill — inputs,
labour, fee — and until you commit, nothing is consumed. The craft of this stance is refusing
to commit until the quote and the sale price agree with each other.

## Quick Reference

**A trip is worth taking when** it ends with the inputs of a recipe you have already quoted,
sitting at the station where the craft will happen, for less than buying them there. That is
the only thing a trip does for you tonight. Mining your own inputs beats buying them when the
belt carries what the recipe wants and the hold is big enough to make the fuel worth it;
buying beats mining whenever the market here is deep and the ore is three jumps away. Decide
that per input, not per recipe: most lines are mixed.

**Walk away when** you have not quoted the recipe, when you do not know what the
output sells for, when the spread is smaller than the fuel and service the trip will cost, or
when finishing it would leave you unable to pay the fee at the far end.

**Counters you read first when docked:**

- **Workshop** — what the job consumes and escrows, and whether it can be handcrafted here.
- **Market** — what the inputs cost *here*, with depth, and what the output sells for.
- **Storage** — what you already own, here and at the other stations you have used.
- **Facilities** — handcrafting needs a workshop; the bigger routes need a facility.
- **Obligations desk** — escrow plus accruing tax is what crosses your credit margin.

## Procedure

*You are docked with a quote in hand: the recipe wants sixty units of an ore the market here
sells at a price that eats most of the margin, and a belt two jumps out carries it.* The trip
pays for itself if one hold covers the sixty and the round trip costs less than the spread.
Cautious, with fuel near the margin, the better choice is the smaller one: buy what the market
has, make a shorter run of the recipe, and keep the credits for the fee.

*The last outcome came back with the ore held rather than deposited.* This station has no store
for it, so the inputs are aboard and the hold is the constraint now. Do not fetch more. Move to
a station that will take the deposit, or take the craft where the cargo already is.

*You have everything the recipe needs and the output's sell price has fallen since you quoted.*
The inputs are not wasted — they are inventory. Read the market again, and if the buyer is
gone, hold them rather than committing a craft into a price you did not accept.

## Pitfalls

- Ore in storage is not credits. Do not plan a purchase against it until it has been sold.
- A remote listing never proves what a deposit holds; resources are confirmed after arrival.
- A recipe quoted at one station is not quoted at another; fees and routing differ.
- Recycling recovers a fraction. It is a way out of a mistake, not a plan.
- Committing consumes the inputs. An unsettled craft is worse than an unstarted one.

## Verification

The objective is met when what you hold equals what the objective named, read from the game
rather than remembered.
