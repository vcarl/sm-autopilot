# SpaceMolt Industrialist Skill

The evening you are having is "I'll make something." You read what a recipe wants, get the
inputs — bought where they are cheap, or mined yourself — and settle at the bench. The money
is made in the gap between what the inputs cost you and what the output sells for, and that
gap is decided before you ever leave the station.

## When to Use

At every juncture of an Industrialist shift, beside the shared `spacemolt` skill.

## Prerequisites

The shared skill. This stance opens Cautious or Focused; both suit work whose profit is
arithmetic done in advance rather than a chance taken in flight.

## How to Run

Quote first, then decide, then move. A quote costs nothing and tells you the real bill — the
inputs, the labour and the fee — and until you commit, nothing is consumed. The whole craft
of this stance is refusing to commit until the quote and the sale price agree with each other.

## Quick Reference

**A trip is worth taking when** it ends with the inputs of a recipe you have already quoted,
sitting at the station where the craft will happen, for less than buying them there. That is
the only thing a trip does for you this evening. Mining your own inputs beats buying them when
the belt carries what the recipe wants and your hold is big enough to make the fuel worth it;
buying beats mining whenever the market here is deep and the ore is three jumps away.

**It is not worth taking when** you have not quoted the recipe, when you do not know what the
output sells for, when the spread is smaller than the fuel and service the trip will cost, or
when finishing it would leave you unable to pay the fee at the far end.

**Counters you read first when docked:**

- **Workshop** — the recipe and the quote. Look at what the job will consume, what it escrows,
  and whether it can be handcrafted here or has to route to a facility. A craft leaving the
  queue is not by itself proof of finished output; read the output.
- **Market, buy side** — what the inputs cost *here*, with depth, not a remembered price.
  Then the sell side for the output, because a recipe that cannot be sold is a hobby.
- **Storage** — what you already own, at this station and at the others. Half of industry is
  discovering you bought these inputs two shifts ago and left them somewhere.
- **Facilities** — what exists here at all. Handcrafting needs a workshop; the bigger routes
  need a facility, and building one is the largest single spend in the game.
- **Obligations desk** — tax accrues with nothing behind it. Escrow plus tax is what takes you
  under your credit margin while you are watching the bench.

**Moods.** Cautious buys inputs it can afford to lose and quotes twice; it prefers the recipe
with a known buyer over the one with a better margin. Focused finishes the crafting line it
started and lets the interesting recipe wait for the next shift. If Opportunistic finds you, it
is for a market that moved, not for a new recipe — a half-stocked line is inputs stranded.

**Go home and resupply when** your credits are close to what the next fee and the accruing tax
will take, when the hold is full of inputs and the remaining ones are only buyable at the
bench station anyway, or when a quote you relied on has moved against you and you need to
re-read the market from a dock rather than from a belt.

## Procedure

1. **Read the objective as a quantity with an end.** "Two hundred plates in storage" is done
   when storage says so. Subtract what the last outcome delivered; the remainder is the job.
2. **Quote the whole line before you buy the first input.** Inputs, labour, fee, and the sale
   price of the output. If any of those is a guess, the next act is a read, not a purchase.
3. **Decide buy-or-mine per input, not per recipe.** Most lines are mixed: the common ore is
   cheaper to dig, the refined component is cheaper to buy.
4. **Commit only what you can settle.** Committing consumes the inputs. An unsettled craft is
   worse than an unstarted one.

### Worked junctures

*You are docked with a quote in hand: the recipe wants sixty units of an ore the market here
sells at a price that eats most of the margin, and a belt two jumps out carries it.* The trip
pays for itself if one hold covers the sixty and the round trip costs less than the spread.
Cautious, with fuel near the margin, the better choice is the smaller one: buy what the
market has, make a shorter run of the recipe, and keep the credits for the fee.

*The last outcome came back with the ore held rather than deposited.* This station has no
store for it, so the inputs are aboard and the hold is the constraint now. Do not fetch more.
Move to a station that will take the deposit, or take the craft where the cargo already is.

*You have everything the recipe needs and the output's sell price has fallen since you
quoted.* The inputs are not wasted — they are inventory. Read the market again, and if the
buyer is gone, hold the inputs rather than committing a craft into a price you did not accept.

## Pitfalls

- Ore in storage is not credits. Do not plan a purchase against it until it has been sold.
- A remote listing never proves what a deposit holds. Resources are confirmed after arrival.
- A recipe quoted at one station is not quoted at another; fees and routing differ.
- Recycling recovers a fraction, never the whole. It is a way out of a mistake, not a plan.

## Verification

The objective is met when what you hold equals what the objective named, read from the game
rather than remembered. Then the evening is over: rest at home. Rest is what ends a shift, and
ending one on a finished line is the best version of this stance, not a failure to find more.
