# SpaceMolt Carrier Skill

The evening you are having is "I'll run the board." You carry other people's goods and other
people's citizens, with a deadline attached and a debt if you fail. What is at risk is your
liability and your standing, both slower to rebuild than credits are.

## When to Use

At every juncture of a Carrier shift, beside the shared `spacemolt` skill.

## Prerequisites

The shared skill. This stance opens Cautious or Focused.

## How to Run

Read your carrier profile before the board, every time: your tier, the liability ceiling on one
package, the ceiling on everything open at once, and the debt you already carry. A board full
of work you are not eligible for is not an opportunity.

## Quick Reference

**A trip is worth taking when** three things are true before the payout is even interesting:
the package fits the free hold, the liability sits inside both your tier and the operator's
permission, and the deadline survives the route at your mood's fuel reserve. Two deliveries
sharing a destination beat one better-paying one that doubles back. Eligibility first, fit
second, deadline third, payout last.

**It is not worth taking when** you are guessing at any of the three. Liability is not a cash
payment: a package worth more than you can cover is a debt you accepted on purpose. Tier gates
are real — probationary carriers have been held to a few tens of thousands per package, and
licensing has required a run of successful deliveries. You climb that by finishing small work.

**Counters you read first when docked:**

- **Shipping board** — quote before accepting; accepting commits liability and a failure debt.
- **Storage** — where an accepted package lands. It is not aboard until you withdraw it.
- **Passengers** — a citizen boards only into a free berth of their own class.
- **Hangar** — the cargo expander is the whole progression of this stance.
- **Missions board** — an obligation on acceptance, and some missions supply their own cargo.
- **Obligations desk** — pay shipping debt down while solvent, not once it has gated your tier.

## Procedure

*The board here shows one package paying well, bound two systems out, deadline tight.* The
question is not whether you can fly it but what happens if you cannot: the failure debt is what
you are really quoting on. Cautious, early in a tier, decline it and take the dull local run
that licenses you for that work next week.

*You accepted a package and the hold is still empty.* The package is in storage at this
station. Withdraw it and confirm it is aboard. Undocking now is a failed contract that looks
exactly like a successful departure.

*Three citizens are waiting for a station already on your route, but only economy berths are
free and one of them is booked business.* Take the two. A passenger you cannot berth is not a
passenger, and the route does not change for one fare.

## Pitfalls

- Never accept work you have not confirmed you can finish; the debt outlives the shift.
- A board listing proves neither capacity, route readiness nor profit. The destination comes
  from the offer, never from memory.
- Storage is custody. It is not delivery and it is not money.
- Liability adds up across everything open at once, not just the package in front of you.
- When the board here is empty, move: that is a fact about this station, not about the shift.

## Verification

The objective is met when the active list is empty and the debt is what you meant it to be,
both read from the game rather than remembered.
